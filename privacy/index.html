MIVLA™ Privacy Policy

Informational Risk Alert System

Version: 1.2 Last updated: 13 May 2026 Effective: Upon app release

Our core privacy commitment: MIVLA analyses content entirely on your device. Screen captures used for analysis are taken only after your explicit Android system consent, are processed in memory, and are discarded immediately after the result is shown. Content is never uploaded to our servers, never transmitted to third parties, and never stored. The AI models that detect generated content run locally inside the app.

2. What MIVLA does

MIVLA helps you assess whether images, videos, and texts you encounter on social media are likely to have been generated by artificial intelligence, and what level of risk each piece of content represents for sharing. MIVLA delivers this assessment in two ways:

2.1 Automatic real-time overlay on five supported platforms

When MIVLA's Accessibility Service and screen capture session are both active, and you are using Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, or X (formerly Twitter), MIVLA observes content as it appears on your screen during normal scrolling. For each piece of content, MIVLA displays two indicators overlaid on your screen: an "AI" badge if the content is likely AI-generated, and a risk semaphore (green, yellow, or red) suggesting how cautious to be about sharing it. You do not need to take any action — the indicators appear automatically.

2.2 On-demand analysis on any other app

Outside of the five supported platforms, you can trigger an analysis by pressing and holding any image, video frame, or text on your screen for a few seconds. MIVLA captures the relevant area through the same screen capture session you previously authorised, runs the same analysis, and shows the same indicators.

In both cases, MIVLA's on-device analysis combines a neural network (CLIP-based detector), on-device optical character recognition (Google ML Kit Text Recognition), and metadata inspection. After the result is displayed and the overlay is dismissed, MIVLA discards the analysed data from memory.

5. Permissions MIVLA requests

5.1 Accessibility Service

MIVLA uses the Android Accessibility Service for a single, narrowly scoped purpose: detecting which app is currently in the foreground, so that the automatic overlay described in Section 2.1 can be activated when one of the five supported platforms (Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Facebook, X) is open.

Critically, MIVLA's Accessibility Service reacts only to TYPE_WINDOW_STATE_CHANGED events (foreground app changes). It does not read the contents of the accessibility tree, does not intercept gestures or input, does not log, record, transmit, or retain the information it observes. Its sole output is an internal signal indicating whether the foreground app is one of the five supported platforms.

You may revoke this permission at any time in Android Settings → Accessibility. MIVLA will continue to function in a limited capacity (the share-menu fallback described below will still work).

5.2 Display over other apps (SYSTEM_ALERT_WINDOW)

MIVLA requests permission to draw over other apps so that the semaphore and AI badge can be displayed as a draggable overlay above the content you are viewing. The position of the overlay is saved locally on your device (in SharedPreferences) so that it persists across sessions. No screen content is captured by this permission alone — it is purely a display permission.

5.3 Screen capture (MediaProjection)

For the actual visual analysis, MIVLA uses the Android MediaProjection API, which captures the contents of your screen. This permission is governed by Android's own consent dialog: you will see a system-generated screen each time you start a MIVLA capture session, asking whether you wish to allow MIVLA to capture your screen. This is an Android-mandated dialog, not one we control, and it cannot be bypassed.

While a capture session is active, an Android system icon appears in your status bar indicating that screen capture is in progress. You may stop the session at any time by tapping that icon, by stopping the MIVLA service from its persistent notification (see 5.4), or by closing the app.

What MIVLA does with the captured frames: each frame is fed to the on-device AI detector, the OCR module, and the EXIF inspector. After the analysis result is displayed on screen, the captured frame is discarded from memory. No frames are saved, no frames are uploaded, no frames are shared. The model bundled with the app does not have the ability to transmit images anywhere — it is a static neural network running locally.

5.4 Foreground service with persistent notification

To keep the screen capture session and overlay responsive while you scroll or switch between apps, MIVLA runs a foreground service. Android requires foreground services to display a persistent notification, which you will see in your notification shade while MIVLA is active. You can pause or disable the service from the notification or from within the app.

5.5 Internet

Used solely to communicate with Google Play Billing for managing optional Premium subscriptions. No MIVLA-specific server is contacted; no image, video, text, or analysis data is transmitted over the internet at any time.

5.6 Boot completed

Used so that the MIVLA service can restart automatically after the device reboots, restoring the automatic-overlay behaviour you had configured. The MediaProjection session does not survive a reboot for security reasons — you will be asked to re-grant screen capture consent after each boot.

5.7 Access to shared content (share menu)

As a fallback for users who prefer not to enable Accessibility Service or MediaProjection, MIVLA also accepts content shared via the standard Android share mechanism. This permission is scoped per share and expires when MIVLA closes.

9. Security

Because MIVLA processes all content locally and does not transmit data to any server, the attack surface is intentionally minimal. There is no MIVLA database to breach, no MIVLA account to compromise, no MIVLA backend to intercept. The neural network model bundled with the app is protected by standard Android application sandboxing and Play Integrity verification.

Both privileged permissions used by MIVLA — Accessibility Service and MediaProjection — are governed by Android operating system protections: Accessibility requires manual activation in system Settings, and MediaProjection requires an explicit system consent dialog at the start of each capture session. Neither permission can be enabled silently by the app.

10. International data transfers

Because we do not collect or transmit personal data, no international data transfers occur in the operation of MIVLA. If you subscribe through Google Play Billing, transfers related to that transaction are governed by Google's own privacy policy and standard contractual clauses.
